# Hidden premise

**A hidden premise is an assumption that an argument needs in order to work but does not state.** It is also called a *tacit premise* or an *assumed premise*; from Unit 2 the syllabus speaks of "hidden premises or assumptions". In [standard form](standard-form), a hidden premise is written in and labelled (HP).

## Why premises go unstated

Often a premise is left out because it seems obvious or is widely shared. Sometimes, though, the unstated premise is the controversial part of the argument. Writing it in lets you see it and evaluate it.

## Example

"Those biscuits must be good because Jessica baked them."

```sf
1. Jessica baked the biscuits.
C. Therefore, the biscuits are good.
```

The argument relies on something unstated about Jessica:

```sf
1. Jessica baked the biscuits.
2. Jessica is skilled at baking biscuits. (HP)
C. Therefore, the biscuits are good.
```

## Choosing the hidden premise

Ask: what else must be true for the stated premises to support the conclusion? There is often more than one candidate. "Everything Jessica bakes is good" would also do the job, but it is a stronger claim and less likely to be true. The *principle of charity* says to reconstruct an argument as strongly as you can: supply the most plausible premise that the argument needs. Your evaluation then targets the argument's best version, not a weaker one.

A hidden premise is especially common where a [normative](descriptive-normative) conclusion is drawn from descriptive premises. "Cigarette smoking causes ill health, so the government should ban it" needs a normative premise, such as "The government has a duty to protect public health" (see [is-ought fallacy](is-ought)).

## A common mistake

Do not simply add "If the premises are true, then the conclusion is true." That premise makes any argument [valid](valid), so it hides the real assumption instead of identifying it. Look for the specific claim the argument relies on.

## Hidden conclusions

Sometimes the premises are stated and the conclusion is only implied. Write the conclusion in and label it (HC):

```sf
1. Eating junk food every day leads to health problems like obesity and diabetes.
2. Junk food is high in fat, sugar, and salt.
C. Therefore, you should avoid eating junk food every day. (HC)
```

Implied conclusions can be vague, so state the one the arguer most plausibly intends.
