## Proposed scope (250–400 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland p. 70): a fallacy is a flaw in reasoning. A formal fallacy is an error in an argument's form, making a deductive argument invalid (link affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent). An informal fallacy is an error in content or in how the reasoning is expressed; it can occur in deductive or inductive arguments.
2. **The families** (Bland ch. 4): relevance, weak induction, presumption, ambiguity/misattribution. Noted as one way of grouping them; others exist. Labelled map placing all 15 entries (14 + straw man).
3. **Identifying fallacies fairly** (Bland p. 86): a fallacy is a matter of the reasoning, not of whether the conclusion is true or popular; a true conclusion can rest on poor reasoning; interpret charitably first; with weak induction it is often a judgement call; arguments sit on a spectrum of strength.
4. **Related**: argument, valid, inductively strong and weak, affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent, all 15 fallacy entries, cognitive bias.
