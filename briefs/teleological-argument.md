## Proposed scope (1,500–2,200 words, in tabs)
1. **The argument (tab)**: arguments from order or purpose (*telos*) in nature to a designer. Versions:
   - Paley, *Natural Theology* (1802) ch. 1: the watch on the heath. Extracts with glosses; Paley's own answers to objections (e.g. a watch that was imperfect, or whose maker we never saw) from chs 1–3 if useful.
   - Aquinas's Fifth Way (governance of the world), one paragraph.
   - Fine-tuning: physical constants within narrow life-permitting ranges; design as the best explanation (inference to the best explanation, explained in one sentence). Sourced from SEP "Fine-Tuning"; defenders paraphrased (e.g. Swinburne, Collins).
2. **Standard form and map (tab)**: Paley as an argument from analogy using the pattern in `analogy` (linked, not restated); fine-tuning as an inductive inference to the best explanation. Classify: inductive. Argument map.
3. **Objections and replies (tab)**:
   - Hume, *Dialogues* II, V, VII: disanalogy between the universe and a machine; we have no experience of universes being made; the inference gives no reason for one, perfect, or good designer — replies (Paley's; that limited conclusions still count).
   - Evolution by natural selection (Darwin, 1859) explains biological adaptation without a designer — replies (design at the level of laws and constants; fine-tuning is not answered by evolution). Stated as a debate, not a verdict.
   - Fine-tuning: the multiverse; the anthropic principle (we could only observe a life-permitting universe) — replies.
   - Faulty analogy? (link `faulty-analogy`, which says it "is judged by the same test").
4. **Evaluating it (tab)**: the five criteria (truth, relevance, number, diversity, disanalogy) applied to Paley showing what each side argues for each criterion, with no overall rating; for fine-tuning, what would make the inference stronger or weaker. No verdict.
5. **Questions to explore** (section Q7): religion and science.
6. **Related**: analogy, faulty-analogy, induction, strong-weak, hypothesis (Section 8), cosmological, ontological, problem-of-evil (design and suffering).
