## Proposed scope (150–350 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland §1.4 pp. 17–18): a deductive argument aims to guarantee its conclusion: if the premises are true, the conclusion must be true.
2. **What makes it deductive**: the premises are logically linked and each contributes; support depends on form (structure), not content (Bland p. 18).
3. **Worked example**: an Australian example in an `sf` block, symbolised with a dictionary, identified as modus ponens.
4. **How it is judged**: valid/invalid, then sound/unsound; all-or-nothing (one line each, linking out, not re-explained).
5. **Where it is used**: mathematics (Bland's Pythagoras proof, pp. 21–23, mentioned only), the named valid forms, and Popper's falsification step.
6. **Related**: argument, induction, deductive vs inductive, valid, sound, classifying arguments, limitations of deduction, the five valid forms.

Out of scope (linked instead): the comparison with induction (`deductive-vs-inductive`); evaluation detail (`valid`, `sound`); limitations (`limitations-of-deduction`).
