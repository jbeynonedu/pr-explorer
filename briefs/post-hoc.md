## Proposed scope (400–600 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland pp. 80–81 and fn. 19): inferring that A caused B on insufficient grounds. *Post hoc ergo propter hoc* ("after this, therefore because of this"): because B followed A. *Cum hoc ergo propter hoc* ("with this..."): because A and B occur together. "False cause" names the wider family.
2. **The pattern**: `1. B happened after A.` / `C. A caused B.`; correlation version: `1. A and B tend to occur together.` / `C. A causes B.`
3. **What else could explain it**: coincidence; reverse causation; a common cause; a condition that would have changed anyway (e.g. recovery from a cold).
4. **Examples** (2–3): a sporting superstition (Queensland setting); a remedy taken before a cold cleared; a correlation with a common cause, sourced if presented as a real statistic.
5. **When it is not fallacious**: sequence and correlation are genuine evidence of causation and often where inquiry starts. A causal conclusion becomes reasonable when alternatives are ruled out (controls, repeated testing, a known mechanism). Link cause and the hypothetico-deductive method; one or two lines only.
6. **Related**: cause, induction, hasty generalisation, slippery slope, hypothetico-deductive method.
