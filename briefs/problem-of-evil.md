## Proposed scope (1,500–2,200 words, in tabs)
1. **The argument (tab)**: the problem stated (section Q10: "evil" in the broad sense, including all suffering; link `evil`, `suffering`). Hume, *Dialogues* X, the passage he gives as "Epicurus's old questions" (extract and gloss; note on the uncertain attribution, sourced). Moral and natural evil (link `evil`). The logical problem (an inconsistency) vs the evidential problem (evil makes God's existence improbable).
2. **Standard form and map (tab)**:
   - Logical: the inconsistent triad (God is omnipotent; God is wholly good; evil exists) plus the hidden premises Mackie adds (a good being eliminates evil as far as it can; there are no limits to what an omnipotent being can do). Symbolised; inconsistency of the full set checked by code. Classify: deductive.
   - Evidential: Rowe's argument (instances of intense suffering a good, all-powerful being could have prevented without losing a greater good), paraphrased; Rowe's fawn. Classify: inductive (or deductive with an inductively supported premise; sourced).
   - Argument map.
3. **Objections and replies (tab)** (theodicies and defences; defence vs theodicy defined):
   - Free will defence (Plantinga): a world with free creatures who sometimes do wrong may be better than one without — replies (natural evil; why not free creatures who always choose rightly).
   - Soul-making (Hick, after Irenaeus): suffering as the condition for moral growth — replies (amount and distribution of suffering; animals; children).
   - Augustine: evil as privation and the result of the Fall — replies.
   - Sceptical theism: we should not expect to see God's reasons — replies.
4. **Evaluating it (tab)**: the logical version: is the set really inconsistent, given the hidden premises? The evidential version: how strong is the inference from "we see no reason" to "there is none"? Where each side places the burden. No verdict.
5. **Related**: evil, good, benevolence, omnipotence, omniscience, god, free-will, suffering, hidden-premise, valid, strong-weak.
