## Proposed scope (150–350 words)
1. **Definitions** (Bland §1.1, pp. 10–11): descriptive (factual) propositions say what *is*; normative propositions say what *ought* to be, and include value judgements (Bland labels them "Normative (Evaluative)"). Bland's examples.
2. **How to tell them apart**: signal words (should, ought, must, good, wrong, better), with the caveat that wording is a guide only ("must" can be descriptive; "is wrong" is normative).
3. **Mixed arguments**: a normative conclusion needs at least one normative premise; link to *is-ought fallacy* and *hidden premise* (Bland's smoking example, p. 14, one line).
4. **Truth and normative claims**: the Proposition entry already treats normative statements as propositions and notes non-cognitivism, linking here. This entry gives the even-handed two-line version: cognitivists hold that moral claims are true or false; non-cognitivists deny they are true or false in any substantial sense. No verdict.
5. **Related**: proposition, claim, hidden premise, is-ought fallacy, morality, value.

Out of scope for this entry (linked instead): analytic/synthetic (Bland pairs it here; see question 1); metaethics in detail.
