## Proposed scope (1,500–2,200 words, in tabs)
1. **The argument (tab)**: what makes it ontological: a priori, from the concept of God alone. Define *a priori* and *a posteriori* here once (other argument entries link to this). Anselm (dates to source), *Proslogion* 2: extract with gloss; ch. 3 (God cannot be conceived not to exist) briefly. Descartes, *Meditation* V: existence belongs to God's essence as three angles summing to two right angles belong to a triangle (wording from the fetched text).
2. **Standard form and map (tab)**: Anselm as a reductio in an `sf` block with hidden premises `(HP)` (e.g. existing in reality is greater than existing in the understanding alone). Classify: deductive. Symbolised skeleton (modus tollens or reductio form), validity by code. Modal version (Plantinga) in English only (section Q4). Argument map (coral).
3. **Objections and replies (tab)**:
   - Gaunilo's perfect island (parody: the same form proves absurd things) — Anselm's reply (the argument applies only to a being than which none greater can be conceived; an island has no intrinsic maximum).
   - Kant: existence is not a real predicate — replies (e.g. that Anselm compares existing in reality with existing in the understanding; modal versions avoid treating existence as a predicate).
   - Begging the question? (link `begging-the-question`) — reply.
   - Modal version: the possibility premise carries all the weight — reply.
4. **Evaluating it (tab)**: the argument is valid on its usual reconstructions, so the dispute is over premises and over whether "existence" works as the argument needs. Shows *how*, no verdict.
5. **Related**: god, a priori (in entry), valid, sound, begging-the-question, hidden-premise, deductive-vs-inductive, cosmological, teleological.
