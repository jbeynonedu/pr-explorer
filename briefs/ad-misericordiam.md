## Proposed scope (300–500 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland p. 73): trying to secure acceptance of a conclusion by arousing emotion, classically pity (*misericordia*), where the emotion is not a relevant reason. The syllabus's English name is wider than the Latin; the entry covers pity as the central case and other emotions (fear, guilt, outrage) briefly (see Q1).
2. **The pattern**: `1. [Situation that arouses pity/fear].` / `C. P.` where the situation is irrelevant to whether P is true.
3. **Examples** (2–3): an excuse for a late assignment that does not bear on the mark the work deserves; an advertisement that uses fear to sell; the classic court distinction (pity is irrelevant to guilt but may be relevant to sentencing).
4. **When it is not fallacious**: when the emotion tracks a relevant fact, e.g. suffering is a genuine reason in deciding whether to help. Emotion in persuasion is not automatically fallacious; the question is whether it replaces reasons (link justification and persuasion).
5. **Related**: justification and persuasion, ad populum, misuse of argument, cognitive bias.
