## Proposed scope (350–550 words)
1. **Definition**: two conditionals and a disjunction of their antecedents give a disjunction of their consequents (Bland p. 29).
2. **The form**: `P ⊃ Q, R ⊃ S, P ∨ R ∴ Q ∨ S`; standard form beside symbolic form. Note: three premises.
3. **Example**: fresh Australian example with dictionary.
4. **Why it is valid**: the truth table has 16 rows (four letters). The conclusion is false in only four of them, and each has at least one false premise (verified by code). See Q1 for how to show it.
5. **Variations**: the disjunction or conclusion in the other order (Bland Exercise 2.1 d); the simple case where both conditionals share a consequent (`P ⊃ Q, R ⊃ Q, P ∨ R ∴ Q`).
6. **Look-alike forms**: destructive dilemma (`P ⊃ Q, R ⊃ S, ~Q ∨ ~S ∴ ~P ∨ ~R`, valid, the modus tollens counterpart); affirming the consequents (`P ⊃ Q, R ⊃ S, Q ∨ S ∴ P ∨ R`, invalid). Both checked by code.
7. **Evaluating a dilemma**: a constructive dilemma is valid, so it is answered by challenging a premise: deny the disjunction (there is a third option) or deny one of the conditionals. Traditional names: "going between the horns" and "grasping a horn" (if sourced).
8. **Related and sources.**
