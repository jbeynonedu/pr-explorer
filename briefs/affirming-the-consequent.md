## Proposed scope (300–500 words)
1. **Definition**: a formal fallacy: affirms the consequent of a conditional and concludes its antecedent (Bland pp. 31–32).
2. **The form**: `P ⊃ Q, Q ∴ P`; standard form beside symbolic form.
3. **Example**: fresh Australian example with dictionary, and a counterexample in words.
4. **Why it is invalid**: interactive four-row truth table (`ac`, already supported); the counterexample row is P false, Q true.
5. **Variations**: the conditional worded as "Q if P", "P only if Q", "P is sufficient for Q" (Bland Exercise 2.2 b, g); negated parts.
6. **Look-alike forms**: table as in the exemplar; modus ponens is the valid form it is confused with.
7. **When similar reasoning is acceptable**: (a) if the speaker means "if and only if", the argument is valid, but it is a different argument with a different premise (link hidden premise, `(P ⊃ Q) & (Q ⊃ P)`); (b) treated as inductive, "Q, and P would explain Q" can support P without guaranteeing it. Link: testing a hypothesis by its predictions has this shape, so a successful prediction cannot prove a hypothesis (link hypothetico-deductive method; one line).
8. **Related and sources.**
