## Proposed scope (300–500 words; Explain entry, as with standard form)
1. **The core distinction** (Bland p. 19): a deductive argument seeks to guarantee its conclusion; an inductive argument seeks only to make it probable.
2. **Consequences for evaluation**: valid/invalid and sound/unsound vs strong/weak; all-or-nothing vs degrees ("shades of grey", Bland p. 19).
3. **Comparison table**: aim; link between premises and conclusion; criteria; can it come in degrees?; does the conclusion go beyond the premises?; typical uses (mathematics; science and everyday reasoning).
4. **Paired example**: one conclusion argued both ways (Bland's Pythagoras pair, pp. 21–23, or a fresh shorter pair).
5. **Not the difference**: "general to specific" vs "specific to general" is a common but unreliable rule of thumb (flag: needs a source, IEP); "probably" is a cue, not a test.
6. **Neither is "better"** (Bland p. 21): knowledge of the world relies heavily on induction.
7. **Related**: deduction, induction, valid, sound, inductively strong and weak, classifying arguments, limitations of deduction, Hume's problem of induction.
