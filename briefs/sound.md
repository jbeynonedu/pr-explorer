## Proposed scope (250–400 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland p. 18; p. 66): sound if and only if valid and all premises true. Unsound if invalid, or if any premise is false.
2. **What soundness gives you** (Bland p. 19): a sound argument's conclusion must be true. Converse fails: a true conclusion does not make an argument sound (Bland Ex. 3.6 f).
3. **Order of evaluation**: validity first, then premises. Process diagram.
4. **Worked example** (Bland §3.6 pp. 66–67 pattern): a valid modus ponens with one easily checked premise and one contested premise; a fresh, non-partisan Australian example (the minimum-wage example takes a side on a live economic question unless both sides are presented).
5. **Contested premises** (Bland p. 67): the practical standard (plausible and well supported); the four-step method for evaluating a contested premise (state, case for, case against, judgement).
6. **All-or-nothing**: never "mostly sound".
7. **Related**: valid, deduction, true and false, plausible, credible, premise, inductively strong and weak (the inductive counterpart is strength plus true premises; often called "cogent", not a syllabus term).
