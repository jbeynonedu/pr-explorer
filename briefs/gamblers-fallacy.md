## Proposed scope (300–450 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland pp. 79–80): believing that past outcomes of independent random events change the probability of the next outcome (a result is "due").
2. **Independence** (Bland p. 79, fn. 18): the fallacy applies only to independent events. A fair die has no memory: after five 1s in a row, the chance of a 1 is still 1 in 6; other numbers are not "overdue", and the streak is not more likely to continue.
3. **The pattern**: `1. X has not occurred in the last n independent trials.` / `C. X is more likely on the next trial.`
4. **Why it is tempting**: long-run proportions do settle near the probability, but by being swamped by later trials, not by compensating. One worked figure, verified by code.
5. **Examples** (2–3): coin tosses; roulette or lottery numbers "overdue"; a family with several children of one sex. Real gambling cases (e.g. poker machines) only if sourced; the 1913 Monte Carlo story only if a reliable source is found.
6. **When it is not fallacious**: when events are not independent: drawing cards without replacement does change later probabilities.
7. **Related**: induction, hasty generalisation, cognitive bias, inductively strong and weak.
