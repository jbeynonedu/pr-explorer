## Proposed scope (150–350 words)
1. **Framing**: the four terms name what an argument (or a piece of reasoning about arguments) is *for*. Justify and explain are things arguments do; analyse and evaluate are things we do *to* arguments.
2. **Justify**: give reasons for accepting that a claim is true (or an action right). The central purpose of argument.
3. **Explain**: give reasons *why* something is the case, when it is already accepted that it is. The difference from justification: same form ("because"), different job. Example pair: "The road is wet because it rained" (explanation) vs "It rained, because the road is wet" (justification).
4. **Analyse** (QCAA Cognitive verb framework, as quoted in Bland p. 13): break an argument into parts (standard form, hidden premises) to reveal what is not obvious.
5. **Evaluate** (same source): judge its quality: truth of premises, validity/soundness or strength.
6. **Related**: argument, standard form, justification and persuasion, valid, sound, inductively strong.

Out of scope for this entry (linked instead): persuasion (section 6); the cognitive verbs as assessment instructions in general.
