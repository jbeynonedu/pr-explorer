## Proposed scope (450–600 words)
1. **Definition**: the tendency to seek, interpret and remember evidence in ways that favour what one already believes or expects (Nickerson 1998; short quotation or paraphrase).
2. **Three forms**: biased search (looking only for supporting cases), biased interpretation (reading mixed evidence as support), biased memory. One short example each, school or everyday life.
3. **Evidence it exists**: Wason's 2-4-6 task (1960), described accurately; Lord, Ross and Lepper (1979) on both sides rating the same mixed evidence as supporting their own view (section Q6).
4. **Bacon**: one quotation from *Novum Organum* I.46 (Spedding, Ellis and Heath trans.), with a gloss; Bacon (1561–1626).
5. **Link to argument evaluation**: confirmation bias vs a biased sample (link `hasty-generalisation`, `generalisation`'s bias criterion); why seeking counterexamples and falsification matter (one line linking Section 8 on Popper, when it exists).
6. **Questions to explore** (section Q4).
7. **Related**: cognitive-bias, hasty-generalisation, generalisation, strong-weak, justification-persuasion.
