## Proposed scope (300–500 words)
1. **Definition**: a formal fallacy: denies the antecedent of a conditional and concludes the denial of its consequent (Bland pp. 32–33).
2. **The form**: `P ⊃ Q, ~P ∴ ~Q`; standard form beside symbolic form.
3. **Example**: fresh Australian example with dictionary, and a counterexample in words. (The lotto pair, same conditional as modus tollens, shows that one structural change makes the difference: Bland p. 33. Use a fresh pair, not the lotto text.)
4. **Why it is invalid**: interactive four-row truth table (`da`, already supported); counterexample row P false, Q true.
5. **Variations**: "only if" wording, e.g. `K only if F. Not K. ∴ Not F` (Bland Exercise 2.2 d, f); negated parts, e.g. "If the power is out, the lights will not work. The power is not out. So the lights work" (Exercise 2.2 h): `P ⊃ ~L, ~P ∴ L`.
6. **Look-alike forms**: table as in the exemplar; modus tollens is the valid form it is confused with.
7. **When similar reasoning is acceptable**: everyday "if" often means "if and only if" (forall x 5.5: "if you don't eat your greens, you won't get any dessert"); on that reading the argument is valid, because it has a different premise. Link hidden premise and necessary and sufficient conditions.
8. **Related and sources.**
