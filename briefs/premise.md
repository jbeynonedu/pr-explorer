## Proposed scope (150–350 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland pp. 8, 13): a proposition offered as a reason to accept the conclusion.
2. **Identifying premises in prose**: premise indicator words (because, since, given that, for, as shown by); indicators are a guide, not a rule (e.g. "since" can be temporal).
3. **Premises are claims**: each can be challenged; evaluation asks whether each premise is true (and, for deduction, whether together they support the conclusion). Links to sound/strong.
4. **Premises work together**: in many arguments, especially deductive ones, premises support the conclusion jointly, not one by one (Bland p. 18).
5. **Hidden premises** in one line, linking to *hidden premise*.
6. **Related**: conclusion, argument, claim, hidden premise, standard form, sound, inductively strong.
