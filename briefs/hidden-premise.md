# Brief: Hidden premise (term)

## Syllabus dot points (verbatim)

Syllabus p. 7: "breaking down arguments into their constituent components of premise/s and conclusion/s, including assumed premises necessary for an argument to cohere."

## John's unit material

## Proposed scope (150–350 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland §1.3, p. 16): an unstated assumption the argument needs in order to work; also called a tacit premise or assumed premise (syllabus wording). Labelled (HP) in standard form.
2. **Why premises go unstated** (Bland p. 16): often obvious or shared knowledge; sometimes controversial, which is exactly why making them explicit matters for evaluation.
3. **Worked example**: Bland's Jessica's biscuits (p. 16), before and after, in `sf` blocks.
4. **How to find one**: ask what must also be true for the premises to support the conclusion; choose the most plausible premise that does the job (principle of charity, syllabus p. 7). Note that more than one candidate may fill the gap.
5. **Hidden premises and the is-ought gap**: Bland's smoking example (p. 14) as a second worked example; link to *is-ought fallacy*.
6. **Hidden conclusions** (HC), briefly (Bland p. 16).
7. **Related**: premise, conclusion, standard form, descriptive/normative, is-ought fallacy.

Out of scope for this entry (linked instead): the principle of charity as its own topic; the is-ought fallacy in detail (section 4).
