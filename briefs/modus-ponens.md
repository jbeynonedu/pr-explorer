## Proposed scope (300–500 words)
1. **Definition**: affirms the antecedent of a conditional to conclude its consequent; Latin *modus ponendo ponens*, "the method of affirming by affirming" (Bland p. 26).
2. **The form**: standard form beside symbolic form; line form `P ⊃ Q, P ∴ Q`.
3. **Example**: a fresh Australian example in `sf` blocks with dictionary (not the oval example, which modus tollens uses).
4. **Why it is valid**: interactive four-row truth table (the widget already supports `mp`).
5. **Variations**: negated parts (e.g. `~P ⊃ Q, ~P ∴ Q`); conditional worded as "Q if P" or "P only if Q" (Bland p. 26 and n. 6); the conditional premise may come second.
6. **Look-alike forms**: table as in the exemplar; affirming the consequent is the one it is usually confused with.
7. **Where it matters** (optional, see Q1): Lewis Carroll, "What the Tortoise Said to Achilles" (*Mind*, 1895): adding the rule as an extra premise never closes the gap, so a rule of inference is not just another premise. Carroll died 1898: public domain.
8. **Related and sources.**
