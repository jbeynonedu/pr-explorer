# Brief: Proposition (Phase 0 exemplar, term)

## Syllabus dot points (verbatim, Unit 1 Topic 1, p. 11)
- Define and use terminology relating to the composition of argument, including argument, claim, proposition, premise, conclusion
- translate and symbolise propositions using logical operators: negation ( ~ ), conjunction ( & ), inclusive disjunction ( ∨ ), conditional ( ⊃ )

## Proposed scope (150–350 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland §1.1): a declarative statement capable of holding truth value; in classical logic, exactly one of true or false (bivalence, non-contradiction).
2. **Propositions and non-propositions**: questions, commands, exclamations (Bland's table).
3. **Truth value can depend on context** (time, place, dispute), with Bland's examples recast.
4. **Proposition and claim**: how the two syllabus terms relate. *Needs a sourced position; see question 1.*
5. **Simple and compound propositions**, and the dictionary of propositions used before symbolising (link to logical operators).
6. **Related**: claim, premise, conclusion, logical operators, true/false, descriptive/normative.

Out of scope for this entry (linked instead): analytic/synthetic, descriptive/normative, theories of truth.
