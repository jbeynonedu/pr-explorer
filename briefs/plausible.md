## Proposed scope (300–450 words)
1. **Definition** (John, 26 Nov 2026; Bland p. 67): a claim is plausible if it makes sense on face value (*prima facie*): it could well be true, judged from the claim itself. Plausibility does not depend on the source or on supporting evidence; that is credibility. One sentence on the difference from `credible`.
2. **Plausible is not true**: plausibility comes in degrees; it depends on background knowledge, so it can change as knowledge changes; plausible claims can be false and implausible claims can be true. One sourced historical case of an implausible-seeming claim later accepted, or a constructed pair if no clean case is sourced.
3. **Using plausibility in evaluation**: one line and a link to `sound` for the working standard (plausible + weight of evidence); for inductive arguments, plausibility of premises is a question about acceptability, separate from strength (same wording as `strong-weak`).
4. **Example**: two premises from one argument, one plausible and one implausible, with why.
5. **Common mistakes**: treating "plausible" as "proven" or "probably true by the numbers"; calling an argument plausible when meaning strong.
6. **Related**: true-false, credible, claim, sound, strong-weak, premise.

## Scope decision (John, 26 Nov 2026)
Plausible is almost synonymous with possible: it makes sense on face value, and does not lean towards credibility.

Raised with John (to confirm wording): "plausible" is usually stronger than "logically possible". "I will win the lottery every week this year" is possible but not plausible. Proposed definition sentence: "A claim is plausible if it makes sense on face value: nothing about the claim itself makes it hard to believe. This is more than being merely possible, and less than being credible or true." The approved `sound` gloss "(it appears to be true)" is consistent with this.
