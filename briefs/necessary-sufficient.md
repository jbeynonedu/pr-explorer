## Proposed scope (300–450 words)
1. **Necessary** (Bland p. 42): A is necessary for B if B cannot hold without A. Symbolised `B ⊃ A`. Example: water and plant growth (p. 42–43).
2. **Sufficient** (Bland p. 43): A is sufficient for B if A guarantees B. Symbolised `A ⊃ B`. Example: breathing and being alive.
3. **Same conditional, two directions** (Bland p. 43): "A is necessary for B" says the same as "B is sufficient for A".
4. **Necessary and sufficient**: each guarantees the other (Bland: divisible by 2 / even). Notation: see Q1.
5. **Kinds of connection** (Bland p. 42): causal, definitional, rule or convention, evidential.
6. **Wording cues**: "if" introduces a sufficient condition; "only if" introduces a necessary one (needs a source: forall x).
7. **Common mistakes**: treating a necessary condition as sufficient (and the link to affirming the consequent / denying the antecedent).
8. **In philosophical arguments**: one line on Searle (programs not sufficient for minds; links to the existing entry), so Unit 2–4 students see why it matters.
9. **Related**: logical operators, translating and symbolising, affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent, Searle's Chinese room, cause.
