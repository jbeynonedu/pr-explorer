## Consistency with approved entries (do not repeat)
- `deductive-vs-inductive` already has the comparison table, the paired formal example, the "general to specific" correction and "neither is better". Induction links to it; it does not restate them.
- `deduction` defines by aim with one line on the success-based alternative. Induction mirrors this: "aims to make its conclusion probable".
- `sound` says a strong argument with true premises is called *cogent* in some texts. Strong-weak must use the same split: strength is about support; premise truth is a separate question.
- `faulty-analogy` gives the pattern with `A`, `B`, `r1, r2...`, `Q` and a three-step test, and defers the full criteria to `analogy`. Analogy uses the same letters and gives the full criteria.
- `hasty-generalisation` gives the pattern with "group A", "property B". Generalisation should use matching letters (Bland uses P and Q; propose A and B for consistency with the approved entry).
- `gamblers-fallacy`, `faulty-analogy`, `hasty-generalisation` call themselves fallacies of "weak induction" and link to `strong-weak`. Strong-weak gives the short map of these.
- `limits-of-deduction` and `deductive-vs-inductive` link to `problem-of-induction` and name Hume (1711–1776).
