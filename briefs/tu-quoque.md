## Proposed scope (300–450 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland pp. 71–72): rejecting an argument or claim because the person making it does not act consistently with it ("you too"). The fallacy is committed by the one who responds, not by the hypocrite.
2. **Relationship to ad hominem**: traditionally a kind of circumstantial ad hominem (Bland p. 72); given its own entry, cross-linked.
3. **The pattern**: `1. A argues that you should not do X.` / `2. A does X.` / `C. A's argument fails (or: you may do X).`
4. **Examples** (2–3): a doctor who smokes advising a patient to quit (fresh wording); a parent and screen time; a public-figure case only if both-sided or non-political and sourced.
5. **When it is not fallacious**: when the question is about the person, not the claim: their sincerity, whether to trust their testimony, whether they should hold a role. Pointing out inconsistency can also fairly ask someone to apply a standard consistently. None of these shows the original argument is unsound.
6. **Related**: ad hominem, credible, justification and persuasion.
