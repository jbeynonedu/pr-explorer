## Proposed scope (400–550 words)
1. **Definition**: a claim is credible if it is worthy of belief given its support, especially the reliability of its source (section Q1). "Credible" is also said of sources (people, publications); the entry keeps the two uses distinct.
2. **Judging a source**: relevant expertise (link `ad-verecundiam`, do not repeat its list); track record; interest in the outcome (exact line from `ad-hominem`); whether others independently confirm it (corroboration); whether it fits well-established evidence (Bland p. 103, acceptability).
3. **Credibility and argument quality** (section Q2): a source's credibility never changes whether an argument is valid or strong; it matters when you accept a claim on someone's word. Rejecting a claim only because of its source is a fallacy (link `ad-hominem`, `tu-quoque`); accepting it only because of popularity is `ad-populum`.
4. **Hume on testimony**: one short quotation from *Enquiry* §X Part I on weighing testimony against experience, with a plain-English gloss. The miracles argument is not evaluated here.
5. **Worked example**: one claim reported by two sources of different credibility; what each supports.
6. **Related**: plausible, true-false, claim, ad-verecundiam, ad-hominem, tu-quoque, ad-populum, sound.
