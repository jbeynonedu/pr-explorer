# Brief: Argument (term)

## Syllabus dot points (verbatim)
Unit 1 Topic 1, p. 11:
- Define and use terminology relating to the composition of argument, including argument, claim, proposition, premise, conclusion
- Explain how arguments are structured
- Interpret and analyse arguments, ideas and information in order to identify the components of arguments, including premises and conclusions

Syllabus p. 7 (Values of inquiry): "the deconstruction of arguments is a key tool of interpretation and analysis. This involves breaking down arguments into their constituent components of premise/s and conclusion/s, including assumed premises necessary for an argument to cohere." Also p. 7: an analytical essay "has the purpose of establishing a claim or assertion (central thesis) through careful reasoning. In this respect, essays present an argument."

## Proposed scope (150–350 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland §1.2, pp. 8, 13–14): a set of propositions in which one or more (the premises) are offered as reasons to accept another (the conclusion).
2. **The technical sense vs everyday "argument"** (Bland p. 8, father/son exchange recast): a quarrel is not an argument in this sense; use "debate" or "dispute".
3. **Argument vs claim** (Bland p. 14): a claim asserts; an argument gives reasons for it. Short; the full contrast lives in *claim*.
4. **Structure in outline**: premises, conclusion, the inference between them; intermediate conclusions in a chain (one line, link to *standard form*).
5. **Two ways an argument can fail**: false premises, or premises that do not support the conclusion. Pointer only to deduction (valid/sound) and induction (strong/weak); no detail.
6. **Worked example**: Bland's Penny gambling-ads argument (p. 13) recast, in an `sf` block.
7. **Related**: claim, proposition, premise, conclusion, standard form, hidden premise, purposes of argument, deduction, induction.

Out of scope for this entry (linked instead): deductive vs inductive (section 2), validity, soundness, strength, explanation vs argument (in *purposes of argument*).
