## Proposed scope (350–500 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland §5.3 p. 98): an argument by generalisation draws a conclusion about a whole group from observations of a sample of it.
2. **The pattern**: `1. A sample of members of group A have property B.` / `C. Therefore, probably all (or most) members of group A have property B.` Letters match `hasty-generalisation`.
3. **Universal and statistical generalisations**: "all" vs "most" or "78%". One or two sentences.
4. **Criteria** (Bland p. 99): sample size; representativeness; bias; conclusion scope. Worked evaluation of one strong example (fresh Australian survey example, constructed) against all four, then a one-line contrast with a weak one linking `hasty-generalisation` (not repeating its examples).
5. **In science** (if section Q3 approved): one paragraph, one sourced example (e.g. drug trials generalise from a sample of patients, Bland p. 91); polling and confidence intervals in one sentence (Bland p. 98).
6. **A generalisation as a premise**: a general premise is not itself an argument by generalisation (link `hasty-generalisation`'s "common mistake", one line only).
7. **Related**: induction, strong-weak, analogy, hasty generalisation, composition/division, problem of induction.
