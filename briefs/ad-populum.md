## Proposed scope (300–450 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland p. 72): concluding that a claim is true, or an action right, only because many people believe it or do it.
2. **The pattern** (`sf` schema): `1. Most people believe P.` / `C. P.`; bandwagon variant: `1. Everyone is doing X.` / `C. You should do X.`
3. **Examples** (2–3): an advertising claim of popularity used as a reason to buy; a school-life bandwagon; one sourced historical case of a widely held belief later rejected.
4. **When it is not fallacious**: when the conclusion is about popularity itself ("the most-watched program"); conventions that exist because people follow them (which side of the road); many independent, informed judgements as weak inductive evidence (link expert consensus at ad verecundiam). Strength still depends on who the "many" are.
5. **Related**: ad verecundiam, hasty generalisation, credible, inductively strong and weak, justification and persuasion.
