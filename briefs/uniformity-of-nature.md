## Proposed scope (200–350 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland p. 90): the assumption that nature behaves uniformly, so that the future will resemble the past; also called the uniformity principle.
2. **Hume's own wording**: one short quotation from the *Enquiry* §IV and/or *Treatise* 1.3.6 (wording to copy from fetched text only), with a gloss.
3. **Its role**: it works as a hidden premise in inductive arguments from past to future cases. Worked example: an inductive argument in an `sf` block, then the same argument with the principle added as `(HP)`, showing it then becomes deductive in form (verify the validity claim by code if symbolised).
4. **Why it matters**: it cannot be shown deductively or inductively without circularity: two sentences, linking `problem-of-induction` for the argument.
5. **Precision note**: nature is not uniform in every respect; the principle is usually stated as regularities continuing. One sentence, if a source supports it (SEP Henderson).
6. **Related**: problem of induction, induction, hidden premise, strong-weak.
