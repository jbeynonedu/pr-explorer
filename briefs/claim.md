# Brief: Claim (term)

## Syllabus dot points (verbatim)
Unit 1 Topic 1, p. 11:
- Define and use terminology relating to the composition of argument, including argument, claim, proposition, premise, conclusion
- the quality of arguments and claims, including deductively valid, deductively invalid, sound, unsound, inductively strong, inductively weak, true, false, plausible, credible
- Evaluate claims and arguments from a range of sources using appropriate criteria such as truth, validity, soundness and/or strength.

## John's unit material
Bland's text stands in for unit material for all of Unit 1 (John, 25 Nov 2026).

## Proposed scope (150–350 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland p. 14): a statement that asserts something to be true (or false). Per John's decision, a claim is near-synonymous with a proposition: the difference is that a claim is a proposition someone *asserts*.
2. **Claim vs argument** (Bland p. 14): a claim on its own gives no reasons; an argument supports a claim with premises. Bland's "humans lack free will" example.
3. **Claims inside arguments** (Bland p. 14): premises and conclusions are themselves claims, open to challenge in evaluation (Penny example).
4. **Evaluating claims**: the syllabus criteria for claims (true/false, plausible, credible), one line each with links to section 6. Arguments are not true or false (style guide §2).
5. **Related**: proposition, argument, premise, conclusion, true/false, plausible, credible, descriptive/normative.

Out of scope for this entry (linked instead): truth, plausibility and credibility in detail (section 6); justification vs persuasion (section 6).
