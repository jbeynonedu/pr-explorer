## Proposed scope (350–550 words)
1. **Definitions** (Bland pp. 84–85): *composition*: inferring that a whole has a property because its parts have it. *Division*: inferring that each part has a property because the whole has it. One entry, two halves, as the syllabus lists them together.
2. **The patterns**: `1. Each part of W is F.` / `C. W is F.`; `1. W is F.` / `C. Each part of W is F.`
3. **Examples** (2 each): a team of star players is not thereby the best team; each ingredient tastes good, so the dish will; a high-achieving school, so this student is high-achieving; an expensive car, so each part is expensive.
4. **When it is not fallacious**: composition: when the property is additive (Bland p. 84: ten people of 100 kg each exceed a lift's 800 kg limit). Division: when the inference is tentative rather than conclusive and allows for variation among members (Bland p. 84: an author's track record makes the next novel *likely* to be good). The test is whether the property is the kind that transfers, and how strongly the conclusion is put.
5. **Distinguish**: composition (parts → whole) from hasty generalisation (some members → all members).
6. **Related**: hasty generalisation, generalisation, induction.
