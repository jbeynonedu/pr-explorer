## Proposed scope (900–1,300 words, tabs: section Q5)
1. **Overview tab**: argument is *used* when reasons are offered to justify a claim; it is *misused* when the form of argument is used to persuade without justifying (link `justification-persuasion`), or when a good argument is misrepresented. A short toolkit: put it in standard form, find hidden premises, check premises (true, plausible, credible), check support, name any fallacy. Misuse is not the same as disagreement: a claim you reject can still be well argued.
2. **Political discourse tab**: legitimate use (justifying a policy with reasons the public can check). Common misuses, each with a constructed paired example from both sides of a neutral local issue (section Q6): straw man, ad hominem, slippery slope, red herring (one line; not a syllabus fallacy, Bland p. 77 area), the principle of charity as the remedy. No real politicians, parties or live issues.
3. **Advertising tab**: what an advertisement's implied argument is (hidden premise and conclusion: "you should buy this"); misuses linked to existing entries (ad populum, ad verecundiam, appeal to nature, ad misericordiam) rather than re-explained; one new constructed example that combines two; Australian context: misleading or deceptive conduct under the Australian Consumer Law, s 18 (sourced).
4. **Scientific debate tab**: legitimate disagreement in science (competing hypotheses, new evidence, peer review; science as fallible, linking Section 8). Misuses: demanding proof where only strong inductive support is possible (link style guide's "prove" rule, `strong-weak`, `problem-of-induction`); cherry-picking (link `confirmation-bias`); appeal to the wrong authority; manufacturing doubt, with the tobacco industry memo (1969) as the sourced historical case (section Q6).
5. **Questions to explore** (section Q4), one set at the end.
6. **Related**: justification-persuasion, cognitive-bias, confirmation-bias, credible, straw-man, ad-hominem, slippery-slope, ad-populum, ad-verecundiam, appeal-to-nature, ad-misericordiam, strong-weak.
