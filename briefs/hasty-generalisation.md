## Proposed scope (300–500 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland pp. 76–77): drawing a general conclusion about a group from too few cases, or from cases that are not representative.
2. **The pattern** (Bland p. 77): `1. Some members of group A have property B.` / `C. All or most members of group A have property B.` Two elements are essential (Bland p. 77): the argument is inductive (specific cases to a general claim), and the evidence is insufficient for the strength of the conclusion (sample too small, unrepresentative or biased).
3. **Common mistake** (Bland pp. 77–78): a premise that is itself a generalisation is not a hasty generalisation; the fallacy is in an inference drawn in the argument. One short worked example.
4. **Examples** (2–3): judging a year level from two students; a holiday destination from one visit; online reviews from a self-selected sample.
5. **When it is not fallacious**: large, representative samples give inductively strong generalisations (link generalisation, strong and weak). Whether a sample is "too small" depends on how varied the group is.
6. **Related**: generalisation, induction, inductively strong and weak, composition/division, gambler's fallacy, confirmation bias.
