## Proposed scope (300–450 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland p. 78): an argument from analogy in which the similarities are irrelevant to the conclusion, or relevant differences are ignored.
2. **The pattern**: `1. A and B are alike in respects r1, r2...` / `2. A has property Q.` / `C. B has property Q.`; faulty when r1, r2... do not bear on Q, or A and B differ in a way that does.
3. **Testing an analogy**: list the similarities, ask whether they are relevant to the conclusion, then look for relevant differences. Keep short; full criteria belong to the analogy entry (section 5), to avoid repetition.
4. **Examples** (2–3): constructed, from school life, sport or everyday consumer choices. Avoid the household-budget/government analogy unless both sides are presented.
5. **When it is not fallacious**: relevant similarities and no relevant differences make an analogy inductively strong; analogies are also legitimately used to explain rather than to argue (link purposes of argument).
6. **Related**: analogy, induction, inductively strong and weak, hasty generalisation, purposes of argument.
