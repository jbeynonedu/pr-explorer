## Proposed scope (300–500 words)
1. **Definition** (Bland p. 83): arguing that a small or moderate step will *inevitably* lead, through a chain of consequences, to an extreme and undesirable outcome, when the links in the chain are not supported.
2. **The pattern**: `1. If A, then B.` / `2. If B, then C.` / `...` / `n. Z is unacceptable.` / `C. We should not allow A.` Note that the form looks like a hypothetical syllogism plus modus tollens; the problem is the truth of the conditionals, not the form (link both).
3. **Why the chain is weak**: each link is at best probable; the chance of reaching the end is lower than that of any single link. One worked figure, verified by code at Checked.
4. **Examples** (2–3): school life (one late assignment → expulsion); a constructed policy example that takes no side on a live issue.
5. **When it is not fallacious**: when each link is well supported by evidence (Bland p. 83); the argument is then judged as an inductive causal chain, strong or weak. One line on the precedent (line-drawing) version, if sourced.
6. **Related**: post hoc ergo propter hoc, hypothetical syllogism, modus tollens, inductively strong and weak.
