## SUMMARY
- §1: Religious attitudes seem appropriate only if their object is maximally great ("so perfect and splendid that nothing greater is conceivable"); conceptions of maximal greatness vary between traditions. Theistic Vedanta (Ramanuja, 1017?–1137?; Madhva, 1197–1276) treats Brahman/Vishnu as personal, omniscient, omnipotent, omnipresent, all merciful.
- §2: Classical Christian theists add simplicity, timelessness, immutability, impassibility. Timelessness: God "altogether outside of time". Impassibility is debated: Hartshorne argues a perfect being would be responsive to suffering; Anselm, Bernard and Aquinas offer ways to reconcile compassion with impassibility.
- §4: Limited deities: Plato's demiurge (*Timaeus*); process theism (Hartshorne: God's power and knowledge limited but as perfect as possible); J. S. Mill (*Theism*, 1874): evidence suggests a designer of great but limited power and some benevolence; Mill finds the evidence incompatible with unlimited power, intelligence and benevolence.
