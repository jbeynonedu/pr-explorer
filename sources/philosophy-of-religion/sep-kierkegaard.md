- "Fear and Trembling: A Dialectical Lyric by Johannes de silentio" (Chronology, 1843) — the book is pseudonymous.
- "Since Fear and Trembling is probably the best-known and most read of Kierkegaard’s works, the views of its pseudonymous author are often taken to be Kierkegaard’s own. But since Johannes de silentio claims to be an outsider to “faith”, and repeatedly tells us he cannot understand Abraham, this is a dangerous move." (§3.3.1)
- "Calling the incarnation a “contradiction” has suggested to some that Kierkegaard is a radical fideist who sees Christian faith as requiring a rejection of reason by believing what is logically contradictory, a view applauded by some (e.g., Shestov 1936 [1969]), and decried by others (e.g., Pojman 1984). However, it is far from obvious that Kierkegaard saw Christian faith this way." (§3.3.1)
- "Faith and offense are opposite and rival passions, and neither can be fully justified by reason." (§3.3.1)
- "Haufniensis is clear that a morally free action always comes from a “leap” and cannot be seen as necessary (CA 48–51/SKS 4, 353–7)." (§2, on *The Concept of Anxiety*, 1844)

## SUMMARY
- §3.3.1: In *Fear and Trembling*, faith is contrasted with "infinite resignation"; the knight of faith trusts God's promises "in a way that appears to an outsider like Johannes as “absurd”", and receives the finite (Isaac) back. Problem I asks whether the story contains a "teleological suspension of the ethical": Abraham's private relation to God is given priority over the ethical as "the universal"; unlike the tragic hero, he cannot explain himself in publicly shareable terms.
- §3.3.1: Kierkegaard's account of faith is "non-evidentialist" (compared to Reformed epistemology).
- **"Leap of faith" check**: the SEP entry never uses the phrase "leap of faith". "Leap" appears for *The Concept of Anxiety* (free action as a leap). Combined with the Lowrie text (no "leap of faith"), **do not put "leap of faith" in quotation marks as Kierkegaard's phrase.** The entry can say the phrase is popularly associated with him (only if a source says so) or describe the idea without it. - Attribution: *Fear and Trembling* is written under the pseudonym Johannes de silentio; the entry should say "in *Fear and Trembling*" or "Kierkegaard's pseudonym Johannes de silentio", not present every claim as Kierkegaard's own view.
