## EXACT
- "There is a class of metaethical and normative views that commonly goes by the name ‘divine command theory.’ What all members of this class have in common is that they hold that what God wills is relevant to determining the moral status of some set of entities" (lead)
- "To be a theological voluntarist with respect to some moral status is to hold that entities have that status in virtue of some act of divine will." (§1)
- "It is also an extraordinarily popular charge against theological voluntarism that it entails, objectionably, that morality is arbitrary." (§3.2)

## SUMMARY
- Lead: the label "theological voluntarism" (after Quinn 1990) covers views appealing to the divine will, of which commanding is only one version.
- §1.1: One need not be a theist to be a theological voluntarist (a claim about what *would* ground morality).
- §3.1 "Theological voluntarism and God's goodness": if goodness depends on God's will, saying "God is good" risks becoming empty; replies vary with how much of morality the view claims to explain (e.g. restrict the view to obligation and keep goodness independent; or understand God's goodness as being loving, or as metaphysical goodness). "the strain needed to answer the charge becomes greater the wider the range of normative properties" (SUMMARY of the closing sentence of §3.1).
- §3.2 Arbitrariness: if God's commands could have been otherwise, morality seems arbitrary; the "horrendous commands" objection (if God commanded torture, torture would be obligatory). Standard response: limit divine discretion (God's commands flow from God's nature, so horrendous commands are impossible), allowing "pockets" of discretion; critics reply that the counterfactual still counts against the view (Wielenberg, Morriston; replies by Evans, Flannagan and others).
- Drafting note: the Euthyphro wording is from Plato (`plato-euthyphro.md`); the SEP entry supplies the two horns' difficulties (arbitrariness; emptiness of "God is good") and the main reply (grounding in God's nature). The SEP pages searched did not set out "the Euthyphro dilemma" under that name; do not attribute that phrasing to the SEP.
