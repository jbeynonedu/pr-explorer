## EXACT (copied from the Project PDF text)
- "Omnipotence is maximal power. Maximal greatness (or perfection) includes omnipotence." (lead)
- "Could an omnipotent agent create a stone so massive that that agent could not move it? Paradoxically, it appears that however this question is answered, an omnipotent agent turns out not to be all-powerful." (§1)
- "If the notion of omnipotence were found to be unintelligible, or incompatible with moral perfection, then traditional Western theism would be false." (§1)

## SUMMARY
- §1: Two senses. (1) Power to bring about any state of affairs whatsoever, including impossible ones; Descartes "seems to have had such a notion"; Aquinas and Maimonides held it incoherent; Earl Conee (1991) defends it. (2) Maximal power: no being could exceed it; the entry adopts this sense. Power = ability plus opportunity.
- §2, the stone (agent "Jane"): if she can make an unmovable stone, there is something she cannot do (move it); if not, there is something she cannot do (make it). Resolution 1 (essentially omnipotent agent): "a stone its maker cannot move" is impossible for such an agent, and omnipotence does not require bringing about impossible states of affairs. Resolution 2 (accidentally omnipotent): she could make the stone, then cease to be omnipotent — both at different times.
- §2: Even "any contingent state of affairs" is too strong: e.g. changing the past; another agent's free choice (if libertarianism is true).
- §5: Omnipotence and moral perfection: Pike (1969) argues they conflict (God cannot do evil); replies include that omnipotence does not require the power to bring about every state of affairs another agent can, and Plantinga's free-will view. "This question is central to the problem of evil for theism." (link `problem-of-evil`).
