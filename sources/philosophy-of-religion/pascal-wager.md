## EXACT
- "‘Either God is or he is not.’ But to which view shall we be inclined? Reason cannot decide this question."
- "How will you wager? Reason cannot make you choose either, reason cannot prove either wrong."
- "Yes, but you must wager. There is no choice, you are already committed."
- "Let us weigh up the gain and the loss involved in calling heads that God exists. Let us assess the two cases: if you win you win everything, if you lose you lose nothing. Do not hesitate then; wager that he does exist."
- "But there is an eternity of life and happiness."
- (objector) "I am being forced to wager and I am not free; I am being held fast and I am so made that I cannot believe. What do you want me to do then?"
- (reply) "Concentrate then not on convincing yourself by multiplying proofs of God’s existence but by diminishing your passions."
- "They behaved just as if they did believe, taking holy water, having masses said, and so on. That will make you believe quite naturally"

## SUMMARY
- The argument is prudential: reason cannot settle whether God exists; you must choose; staking a finite life for an infinite gain is rational given even one chance in your favour. Pascal anticipates the objection that belief cannot be chosen and replies that one can act as believers do until belief follows.
- Objections to source before drafting (SEP "Pascal's Wager" or IEP): many gods; belief not voluntary; whether God would reward belief so formed.
