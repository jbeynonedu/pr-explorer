- Antony Flew, "Theology and Falsification". First published 1950 in *University* (Oxford); reprinted in Flew and MacIntyre (eds), *New Essays in Philosophical Theology* (SCM Press, 1955) (Flew, *Philosophy Now* 29, "Theology & Falsification: A Golden Jubilee Celebration", via WebSearch). ## TOOL-EXACT (WebSearch snippet of the CUNY text; re-verify against a fetched copy before quoting)
- "But what remains of your original assertion? Just how does what you call an invisible, intangible, eternally elusive gardener differ from an imaginary gardener or even from no gardener at all?"

## SUMMARY
- Two explorers find a clearing with flowers and weeds; one says a gardener tends it, the other denies it; watches, an electric fence and bloodhounds detect nothing; the believer qualifies the claim (invisible, intangible, insensible to shocks) until the sceptic asks the question above.
- Flew's point (Law 2023): if a believer allows nothing to count against a claim, the claim may assert nothing; "death by a thousand qualifications" (Wikipedia summary; verify wording against the text before quoting).
