## EXACT (copied from the Project PDF text)
- "Omniscience is the property of having complete or maximal knowledge. Along with omnipotence and perfect goodness, it is usually taken to be one of the central divine attributes." (lead)
- (D1) "S is omniscient =df for every proposition p, if p is true then S knows p." (§1)
- (D3) "S is omniscient =df for every proposition p, if p is true then S knows p, and there is no proposition q such that q is false and S believes q." (§1)
- Boethius's view: "God’s perspective is that of eternity, that is, “the complete possession all at once of illimitable life”. In other words, God sees everything that ever happens all at once, so he does not, strictly speaking, know things ahead of time." (§3)

## The foreknowledge argument as SEP sets it out (§3), EXACT
> (1) If God has foreknowledge that S will do A, then it is necessary that S will do A.
> (2) If it is necessary that S will do A, then S is not free with respect to doing A.
> Therefore, (3) If God has foreknowledge that S will do A, then S is not free with respect to doing A.

## SUMMARY
- §1: Variants of the definition (D2, D3) are equivalent to D1 on plausible assumptions. Restricted accounts (Langtry 2008; Nagasawa 2017); Zagzebski's "omnisubjectivity".
- §2: Further features: infallibility; essential omniscience; whether God's knowledge is occurrent or dispositional; Aquinas: God's knowledge is not "discursive" (sees all things together, not by inference).
- §3: Early discussions: Augustine (*On Free Choice of the Will* III.3) and Boethius (*Consolation* V). Boethius accepts the argument but denies that omniscience includes **fore**knowledge (eternity). Aquinas (*Summa contra Gentiles* I.67) finds an ambiguity in premise (1): "necessity of the consequence" (true, but then the argument is invalid) vs "necessity of the consequent" (valid, but the premise is false). A second, harder argument (Pike 1965) uses the fixity ("accidental necessity") of the past; responses: Ockhamists (some facts about God's past beliefs are "soft"), Molina, denial of (6) (counterfactual power over the past). Some reply that the eternity response does not dissolve the harder argument (Plantinga 1986, Zagzebski 1991). Open theism (Hasker; Swinburne; Hoffman and Rosenkrantz) limits omniscience: the future free actions of creatures are not there to be known; this makes providence harder to explain.
- §4: Other puzzles: omniscience and immutability (Kretzmann 1966: knowing what time it is now); first-person (de se) knowledge; Grim's Cantorian argument that there is no set of all truths (Plantinga's reply).
