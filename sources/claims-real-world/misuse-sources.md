# Use and misuse of argument: advertising law and the tobacco "doubt" document

## Australian Consumer Law, s 18

- *Competition and Consumer Act 2010* (Cth), Schedule 2 (Australian Consumer Law), s 18. Federal Register of Legislation, Compilation No. 168, compilation date 27 October 2026, Volume 4: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/latest/text/4
- Crown copyright; legislation text may be quoted.

EXACT:

> Part 2‑1—Misleading or deceptive conduct
>
> 18  Misleading or deceptive conduct
>
> (1) A person must not, in trade or commerce, engage in conduct that is misleading or deceptive or is likely to mislead or deceive.
>
> (2) Nothing in Part 3‑1 (which is about unfair practices) limits by implication subsection (1).

(The hyphen in "2‑1" and "3‑1" is a non-breaking hyphen, U+2011, as on the page.)

## ACCC, "False or misleading claims" (consumer guidance)

- Australian Competition and Consumer Commission: https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/advertising-and-promotions/false-or-misleading-claims (read 27 Nov 2026; no date on page). Copyright ACCC: short quotations or paraphrase only.

EXACT:

> It makes no difference whether a business intends to mislead or not.

> Any statement that creates a false impression about goods and services can be breaking the law.

> In some circumstances, failure to disclose information can be misleading. This is particularly the case if a business provides some information to a consumer but doesn't mention important details the consumer should know that are relevant to their decision.

> 'Puffery' refers to wildly exaggerated and vague claims about a product or service that no one could treat seriously. For example, a restaurant claims they have the 'best steaks on earth'. These types of statements are generally not considered misleading.

> check that the overall general impression is accurate

Case study EXACT (a real, sourced, non-partisan example; usable under style guide §5 if wanted):

> In June 2022, Samsung Electronics Australia Pty Ltd was ordered by the Federal Court to pay $14 million in penalties for misleading water resistance claims about its mobile phones. Samsung admitted to the court that its ads misrepresented the water resistance of its phones.

> Samsung published 9 ads online and in-store. The ads showed the use of various Samsung Galaxy phones in pools and sea water.

> Pool and sea water could, in fact, damage the phones by corroding the charging ports.

- SUMMARY for the entry: legal standard ≠ logical standard. The law asks whether conduct creates a false *overall impression*; puffery is generally not misleading in law, but it still gives no reason to believe the claim. An advertisement can be lawful and still a weak argument.

## Tobacco industry: "Doubt is our product" (1969)

- Document: "SMOKING AND HEALTH PROPOSAL", UCSF Industry Documents Library, Truth Tobacco Industry Documents, ID psdw0147 (old TID rgy93f00), ark:/88122/psdw0147, https://www.industrydocuments.ucsf.edu/docs/psdw0147/ . Record: Author "Unknown"; Document Date "1969"; Type "meeting materials; presentation; speech"; Collections "Brown & Williamson Records; Minnesota Documents; Tobacco Industry Influence in Public Policy; Master Settlement Agreement". Record description begins "1969 B&W …".
- A second copy (xhvf0040, Ness Motley Law Firm Documents) has date and author "Unknown".
- Attribution check: Wikipedia ("Tobacco industry playbook") attributes the line to an R. J. Reynolds memo; the UCSF record places the 1969 copy in the Brown & Williamson Records and its description reads "B&W". SourceWatch also says Brown & Williamson. Use: "a 1969 internal document from the tobacco company Brown & Williamson" (UCSF). Do not call it a "memo" by a named author: the record gives no author.

Quotation, from the UCSF catalogue description EXACT (the record's OCR excerpt has errors, e.g. "Sittle", so quote the description):

> Doubt is our product since it is the best means of competing with the 'body of fact' that exists in the mind of the general public. It is also the means of establishing a controversy.

- Note: the scan itself was not opened (PDF viewer); the wording above is the library's transcription in its record. Before approval, open the PDF page at Checked if possible.
- SUMMARY for the entry: the document treats doubt itself as the goal, not the outcome of weighing evidence. This is a misuse of the fallibility of science: no inductive conclusion is certain, so it is always possible to say "not proven" (link `strong-weak`, `problem-of-induction`).
