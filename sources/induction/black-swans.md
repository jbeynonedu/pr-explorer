## Caution
- Wikipedia ("Willem de Vlamingh") says de Vlamingh's crew were not the first Europeans to see black swans: Antonie Caen is reported to have seen them off Bernier Island in 1636. So avoid "first Europeans to see" and "one observation overturned the belief". Safe claim: de Vlamingh's 1697 expedition recorded black swans on the Swan River, which is named after them.
- The European belief that all swans are white needs its own source before the entry states it.

## Wording decision (proposed to John, 26 Nov 2026)
Supported: the swans native to Europe (mute, whooper, Bewick's) are all white; the black swan is native to Australia; de Vlamingh's expedition recorded black swans on the Swan River in January 1697. Use the swan example as an illustrative generalisation ("every swan observed in Europe has been white ∴ all swans are white"), without claiming what Europeans believed or that 1697 was the first sighting.
