## Extracts

> The man who has fed the chicken every day throughout its life at last wrings its neck instead, showing that more refined views as to the uniformity of nature would have been useful to the chicken. EXACT

> Thus our instincts certainly cause us to believe that the sun will rise tomorrow, but we may be in no better a position than the chicken which unexpectedly has its neck wrung. EXACT

> It can never quite reach certainty, because we know that in spite of frequent repetitions there sometimes is a failure at the last, as in the case of the chicken whose neck is wrung. EXACT


## SUMMARY (for the entry)
- Animals and people come to expect that what has happened regularly will happen again (the horse, domestic animals expecting food).
- The chicken fed every day is finally killed by the same man: the crude expectation of uniformity misled it.
- Russell distinguishes the fact that past regularities *cause* expectations from the question whether there is reasonable ground for those expectations.
