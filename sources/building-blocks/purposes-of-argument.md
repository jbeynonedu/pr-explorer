## vancleave-1-3
Matthew Van Cleave, *Introduction to Logic and Critical Thinking*, 2nd edn, §1.3 "Arguments vs. Explanations". CC BY 4.0. Humanities LibreTexts: https://human.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Philosophy/Logic_and_Reasoning/Introduction_to_Logic_and_Critical_Thinking_2e_(van_Cleave)/01:_Reconstructing_and_Analyzing_Arguments/1.03:_Arguments_vs._Explanations (fetched 25 Nov 2026; fetch tool returned short quotations only).

> An argument that attempts to show why its conclusion is true is an explanation.

> in an explanation we assume that what we are trying to explain (i.e., the conclusion) is true.

Supports (paraphrase from fetch): an argument's premises try to establish *that* the conclusion is true; an explanation's premises show *why* it is true; explanations can be treated as a type of argument. Matches John's decision (same structure, different purpose).
**Flag:** the distinction is itself debated (some philosophers deny explanations are a separate kind from arguments; e.g. a 2012 *Argumentation* paper, Springer doi 10.1007/s10503-012-9288-1, checking only). The entry presents the syllabus/Van Cleave view without claiming it is uncontested.
