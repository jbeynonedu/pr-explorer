## sep-fallacies
Hansen, H., "Fallacies", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 29 May 2015; substantive revision 30 Aug 2024. https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fallacies/ (full text fetched via Chrome, 26 Nov 2026).

§1 item 13 (verbatim):
> The third version of the ad hominem fallacy is the tu quoque. It involves not accepting a view or a recommendation because the espouser him- or herself does not follow it. Thus, if our neighbor advises us to exercise regularly and we reject her advice on the basis that she does not exercise regularly, we commit the tu quoque fallacy: the value of advice is not wholly dependent on the integrity of the advisor.

§2.4 (verbatim): "...the tu quoque ad hominem argument which attempts to deflect a criticism by pointing out that it applies equally to the accuser."
> Recent scholarship suggests that these post-Lockean kinds of ad hominem arguments are sometimes used fairly, and sometimes fallaciously
