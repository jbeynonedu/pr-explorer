Appeal to Nature (Argumentum Ad Naturam)S
The appeal to nature fallacy is another one that is often seen in advertising. For whatever reason, many 
people believe that things that occur in nature carry inherent moral value over those that don’t. Now, at the 
risk of opening a can of worms, it would be an interesting and engaging philosophical inquiry to consider the 
question: “Do natural things have inherent value?” And who knows – perhaps there is even a defensible 
philosophical argument in the affirmative of this question. However, the ad naturam fallacy simply makes the 
mistake of saying “X is good because it is natural”, or “X is bad because it is not natural.” Such categorical 
generalisations can easily be refuted by considering examples to the contrary. For example, earthquakes, 
pandemics, haemorrhoids and melanomas are all naturally occurring things… and you’d be hard-pressed to 
argue that any of these things are particularly good. Likewise, consider human-made inventions such as 
seatbelts, eyeglasses, flushing toilets, anaesthesia, musical instruments… all of these have improved our lives 
and could hardly be thought of as morally bad. When somebody makes a simplistic inference about the moral 
worth of something based on whether or not it is natural, they are committing the ad naturam fallacy.
